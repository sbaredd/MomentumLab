Good. Next we start Setup / Pivot Detection.

Before writing SQL, we should define the feature precisely, just as we did with Base Episode and Base Quality. The first question is:

What exactly should MomentumLab call a Pivot?

For our momentum framework, I suggest we initially define the pivot structurally as the price level that must be exceeded to demonstrate that the stock is leaving its base—not yet as a buy signal.

Then later we can separately evaluate breakout quality: volume expansion, close location, extension from pivot, market/sector conditions, etc.

So our next sequence should be:

Base Episode → Base Quality → Pivot Identification → Breakout/Setup Readiness → Trade Candidate

Let's start with Pivot Identification, without adding scoring yet.

For MomentumLab V1, I propose:

Pivot = the highest meaningful price level formed on the right side of the current base that price must overcome to demonstrate a breakout from that base.

The important point is that the prior swing high is not automatically the pivot. A stock can correct from ₹1,000, form a base, recover, and then develop a tighter resistance area around ₹940. That ₹940 level may become the actionable pivot even though the original swing high was ₹1,000.

So our first Pivot module should identify structure only. It should answer: Where is resistance? When was it formed? How far is current price from it? Has it already been crossed?

I would initially capture fields such as pivot_date, pivot_price, pivot_type, distance_to_pivot_pct, and pivot_broken. We should not yet mix in volume, market regime, sector strength, or Base Quality score. Those belong to breakout/setup qualification.

The first design problem, therefore, is the interesting one:

How should the algorithm discover that ₹940 is the meaningful pivot rather than simply using the ₹1,000 prior swing high?

That is what I suggest we solve next.

That gives us a genuine resistance reference.

There is another important distinction. We can have two pivot types:

BASE_HIGH — the original prior_swing_high, say ₹1,000.

RIGHT_SIDE_PIVOT — a confirmed resistance level created during recovery, say ₹940.

If a valid right-side pivot exists, it can become the actionable pivot. If one doesn't exist yet, the prior base high remains the structural resistance.

This fits our existing architecture nicely because we already calculate confirmed swing highs with anti-lookahead logic. We should reuse that market-structure information rather than inventing another unrelated swing-high algorithm.

Let's define SR07 — Pivot Overhead Supply / Prior Resistance Pressure.

This adds something genuinely different from SR05 and SR06.

The question is:

How much recent price resistance exists immediately around or above the selected pivot?

But on reflection, our pivot itself already comes from structural resistance. Measuring nearby highs again risks duplicating the Pivot layer.

A more useful SR07 for readiness is:

SR07 — Pre-Breakout Price Progression

SR05 tells us how tight the stock is. SR06 tells us whether volume is drying up. SR07 should tell us:

During that tight pre-breakout period, is price actually pressing toward the pivot rather than drifting away from it?

For example, two stocks can both have a 2% five-day range:

Stock A                     Stock B

        Pivot                       Pivot
──────────────               ──────────────
        ╭─╮
     ╭──╯ │
   ╭─╯    │                   ╭╮
 ╭─╯      │                  ╭╯╰╮
╯         │                 ╭╯  ╰──╮
          ↑                        ╰───

Pressing upward              Drifting downward
toward pivot                 away from pivot

SR05 alone considers both "tight." SR07 distinguishes their direction.

I propose two raw measurements over the same five completed sessions before evaluation date:

prebreakout_5d_return_pct
prebreakout_close_position_pct

The first is:

$$ \frac{Close_{D-1}-Close_{D-5}}{Close_{D-5}}\times100 $$

The second measures where the D−1 close sits inside the five-session high-low envelope:

$$ \frac{Close_{D-1}-Low_{5}} {High_{5}-Low_{5}}\times100 $$

Interpretation:

close position near 100% → finishing near the top of the pre-breakout range
near 50% → middle
near 0% → bottom
positive 5-day return → price progressing upward
negative return → price retreating

Again, these are raw observations, not scores. We won't say >80 = good or >3% = good until we have historical evidence.
